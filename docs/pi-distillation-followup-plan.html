Does PI warm-up followed by original-teacher distillation generalize?

TL;DR: first repeat the entire two-stage sequence with three independent PI warm-ups. Compare return-to-original distillation against continued PI training, keeping original and warmed checkpoints as baselines. Then test semantic PI content, unequal contraction of parameter changes, student-generated prefixes and termination behavior. Extend to other models and code only after reviewing replication and cost. The current evidence supports a candidate mechanism, not a general result.

What we know, and what we have not repeated

The original-to-warmed-to-return sequence is θ₀ → seven worked-solution PI updates → θ* → four original-teacher, no-PI updates. Its mean gains over θ₀ were +5.21 / +3.96 / +2.66 percentage points on AIME 2024 / AIME 2025 / AMC23. Both continuation blocks showed gains. However, those blocks shared one warmed checkpoint. They do not provide two independent replications of the whole sequence. The warm-up happened to lower average accuracy; we must not deliberately select degraded warm-ups in the next study.

New completed work, with no GPU launch: I reanalyzed the retained per-question correctness and length metadata. Within each dataset, I averaged the two branch endpoints per question and compared them with the cached original baseline. Endpoints and decision rules fixed before results

The primary endpoint is step-four Avg@8 minus the original checkpoint, reported separately for all three datasets. Secondary contrasts are return-OPD minus continued-PI, and return-OPD minus its own warmed baseline. The warmed baseline is measured independently for each training replicate. No best checkpoint, best seed or favorable benchmark replaces the primary endpoint.

Use distinct evaluation seed banks per replicate, paired across all four conditions within that replicate. Generate each seed from the protocol namespace, replicate seed, question ID and draw; never include method or checkpoint in the hash. This preserves paired comparisons while adding fresh generation noise. Eight draws do not become eight independent training replications.

Retain the user’s practical threshold of +2 percentage points per dataset. As the exploratory replication screen, each dataset should have a mean gain of at least two points and a positive gain in at least two of three independent warm-ups. Report failure or heterogeneity for any dataset; do not replace it with a pooled success label. Three seeds are still a small exploratory sample. If results are promising but uncertain, design a separately budgeted fixed six-seed confirmation; do not automatically extend or repeatedly treat nominal 95% intervals as significance tests.

Resample training replicates and question IDs as crossed clusters, using the same sampled question IDs across replicates and methods because each replicate evaluates the same questions. Pair the baseline seed bank correctly. Report all seed-level scores, seed-only/question-only sensitivity and aggregate intervals. Final confirmation needs sample-size planning from the observed clustered variability and a multiplicity rule; the present design does not promise power for a two-point effect.

A falsifiable theory: unequal contraction of PI-induced changes

Let θ* = θ₀ + δ. PI training might add a mixture of useful and harmful changes. Around θ₀, the original-teacher forward KL on a fixed set of student prefixes has the local approximation below. F is the positive-semidefinite curvature matrix of the teacher-matching loss: in the same-backend softmax model at equality, it is a Fisher-type matrix averaged over those prefixes.

Lreturn(θ₀ + δ) ≈ ½ δᵀFδ
δk+1 ≈ (I − ηF)δk
ΔUk ≈ Σi giδi(1 − ηλi)k

The eigenvalues λ describe how strongly the teacher loss penalizes different directions; g is the local gradient of a smooth held-out utility proxy. If harmful utility components lie in directions that shrink quickly, while useful components shrink slowly, an initially harmful δ can become helpful after a few return updates. Eventually exact return to θ₀ removes the gain. This offers an explanation for a transient student-above-teacher effect without treating the distillation step as a source of new task information: PI supplied information earlier.

Assumptions matter: this is a local approximation on fixed prefixes with detached state sampling, exact same-backend teacher/student distributions and sufficiently small stable steps. Actual Adam has a changing preconditioner, clipping changes the effective step size, and on-policy prefixes change with the student. With a fixed preconditioner P, the analogous update contains PF, not F alone. Hard top-k/top-p boundaries also make actual evaluation utility only piecewise smooth. Therefore this is a hypothesis and a useful mathematical model, not a theorem about the completed runs.

Completed numerical illustration

I checked a two-dimensional toy with δ=(1,1), F=diag(0.1,1), utility gradient g=(1,−2), and η=0.5. The initial utility change is −1. After four steps it is +0.6895, while the KL surrogate falls from 0.55 to 0.0351. A uniform positive shrink of the same initial change stays harmful in this linear utility example.

How to make the termination ablation exact

Let S contain the actual stop-token IDs used by generation. With q the teacher and p the student, the full KL decomposes as:

KL(q‖p) = KLBernoulli(q(S)‖p(S))
+ q(S) KL(q(·|S)‖p(·|S))
+ (1−q(S)) KL(q(·|¬S)‖p(·|¬S)).

For a single stop token, the within-S term is zero. Log all terms with the original sample/token normalization. Stop-only training uses the Bernoulli term; content-only training uses the teacher-weighted conditional non-stop term. Shared-parameter updates can still indirectly change stop behavior, so verify resulting stop probabilities and lengths rather than assuming perfect separation. Record exact response token IDs prospectively for any budget-prefix replay. Decoding then retokenizing old response text is not guaranteed to reconstruct the generated token tape.

Diagnostic evidence to collect before deleting temporary weights

Keep a separate fixed panel of problems and token tapes, independent of all benchmarks and training questions. While each approved model is resident, record per-token teacher KL, entropy, stop probability, base/warmed/current log-probability gaps, valid-token counts and parameter-displacement projections. Any utility-gradient probe uses only the diagnostic panel’s reviewed answers, never evaluation labels. A directional curvature probe or a small number of Hessian-vector products can test the local approximation without forming a full matrix; this needs its own bounded overhead measurement.

Prior work and evidence boundaries

The matched continued-PI control uses our chosen full-vocabulary forward-KL, non-thinking recipe with a deliberate optimizer reset. It is not a full reproduction of every published OPSD/SDFT setting; negative results must retain that scope.

  • Self-Distilled Reasoner: On-Policy Self-Distillation for Large Language Models supplies the privileged-context versus question-only learning setup. Our two-stage return-to-original intervention is a separate hypothesis.
  • Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning, August 2026 revision studies learning from demonstration-conditioned self-teachers while retaining prior capabilities. It motivates measuring both acquisition and retention, rather than treating a single benchmark mean as a complete picture of the warm-up.
  • Agarwal et al., On-Policy Distillation of Language Models: motivates evaluating teacher feedback on student-generated states. It does not establish that our return-to-original sequence beats its teacher.
  • Mobahi et al., Self-Distillation Amplifies Regularization in Hilbert Space: a theoretical regularization analogy with different assumptions; not a transformer convergence proof.
  • Furlanello et al., Born Again Neural Networks: student-above-teacher results with equally parameterized models. Its use of label supervision and different training setup makes it precedent, not a direct reproduction of teacher-only return OPD.
